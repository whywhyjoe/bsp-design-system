Editorial mode — design guide

How to design the warm, employee-facing content register — learning catalogs, resource hubs, awareness pages — on the same tokens, vocabulary and delivery constraints as the app register. An additive layer, not a fork.

Overview

The register, in plain language

Warm, spacious, hero-led. Photography of real working moments. One blue family carrying chrome, links and a single brand device. Big Regular-weight Segoe headings with room to breathe. Calm, plain copy — warmth comes from scale, space and imagery, never from tone.

Everything that makes the app register correct is unchanged: same tokens, same BEM vocabulary, buildless and CDN-free, sentence case, no emoji, red for the logo only. Editorial relaxes the austerity dials — it does not loosen the rules.

Scope

When to use editorial

Editorial informs, orients and welcomes. Apps get work done. They coexist on one page — an embedded applet keeps its austere styling because it simply isn't under .editorial.

The key decision

Showcase vs mesh

Editorial blocks come in two finishes. Pick one per page — the choice is about what the content sits next to, not about taste.

Showcase — the destination finish

Rounded + elevated

8px corners, shadow-4

Lifting cards, shadowed player and carousel shells (heroes stay flush).

For pages you own end-to-end: a learning catalog, a program hub. The page is the product; blocks read as designed objects. --ed-radius (8px), shadow-4, .lift on interactive cards.

Mesh — the flush finish

Flat + hairline

4px or square, no shadow

News rows on dividers, outlined quick links, hairline cards.

For content living beside out-of-box SharePoint web parts (the real T&O Central look). Nothing may read as a designed island. .card--quiet, .player--flat, .key-messages--quiet, plus the mesh-native blocks: .news-list, .quicklink, .related, .webpart.

Don't mix finishes within one surface. A flat news list beside a shadowed, rounded card grid reads as two systems. Per page: pick the finish, apply it to every editorial block. (An embedded app applet is exempt — it keeps app-mode styling.)
Visual language

Hero devices — a kit of parts

One device per hero, never stacked. Full-bleed devices build on .hero.hero--banner; arc builds on the two-column base hero. All heroes are flat/flush — square corners, no elevation — in both finishes.

Blue band.hero--bandDefault. Blue should clearly carry the frame; photo stays full-colour. The BMO Central home treatment.
Scrim.hero--scrimThe photo is the story; you only need text legibility.
Blue overlay.hero--blueThe photo is texture, not subject — the heavier brand wash (100% blue, Multiply, greyscale image at ~40%).

Never: triangle overlays and chevrons (print only) · the red content circle (red is logo-roundel-only) · surrounding-circle icons · decorative gradients — the only sanctioned gradients are the functional legibility scrim and the approved bokeh artwork.

The three-tier art system

The guardrail that keeps warmth from becoming clip-art soup. Photography leads; everything else knows its place.

The prominence rule. When a page has a photo hero, no spot illustration or line-icon appears at competing prominence. A spot illustration as hero is a substitute, never a co-star.

Color rules

Same authority as app mode; editorial adds exactly two sanctioned blue-fill exceptions.

Blue is the unifierChrome, links, accents, one device per page. Still never a random decorative fill.
Sanctioned exceptionsThe scorecard tile and the flood-of-blue band — blessed BMO patterns, white content on top, never interactive controls inside.
Red is logo-onlyThe single red anywhere is the M-bar roundel in the suite bar. Errors use --fg-danger. Never a red content circle.
Lots of whiteEditorial leans on more whitespace than app mode. Tints (--surface-tint-*) alternate section bands; never behind interactive surfaces.
Chart accents are chart-onlyPurple/mint/etc. never decorate pages.
Light onlyThe SP intranet is effectively light-only; editorial does not invest in dark.

Visual language

Shape & elevation — two dials

The register decides both dials at once. Elevation never exceeds the app budget (shadow-4/shadow-8).

Showcase

Cards 8px + shadow-4 · hero media 12px + shadow-8 · .lift rises to shadow-16 on hover.

Mesh

4px or square corners, hairline borders, no shadow, dividers over boxes. Skip .lift entirely.

Visual language

Motion

Functional, never decorative — the app-mode curves and durations. Editorial content carries .reveal; the layer gives it a CSS load-in fade so a page is self-sufficient without the reveal script. .lift is showcase-only. Honour prefers-reduced-motion everywhere.

Practice

Voice — unchanged

Plain, calm, second-person, sentence case, numerals, explicit currency, no emoji, no exclamation marks. If the copy got chattier, editorial went wrong — warmth is scale, space and imagery.

Practice

Do & don't

Do
  • Pick one hero device and one finish (showcase or mesh) per page.
  • Let one photo or one illustration lead; lean on white space.
  • Use content line-icons for labels, Fluent icons for controls.
  • Select photography for BMO's voice: authentic, natural light, human, diverse.
  • Keep an embedded app applet in its own austere register.
Don't
  • Stack brand devices (bokeh + arc + flood on one element).
  • Mix flat and elevated editorial blocks on one surface.
  • Paint blue behind interactive content (scorecard/flood are the only fills).
  • Reach for red, chart accents, decorative gradients, or Dax as a webfont.
  • Redefine :root or invent values a token covers.
